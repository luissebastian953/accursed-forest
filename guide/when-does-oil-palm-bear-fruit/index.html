When does oil palm bear fruit?

Updated 23 October 2026. Prices and costs carry the date of their source. Also in Bahasa Indonesia.

Short answer: oil palm starts bearing fruit in its third year after planting. Improved varieties can put out a first bunch earlier, at about 22 months, but a harvest that earns its keep begins around age three, climbs steeply to age seven, and stays high until about age 20 to 25. After that the palms are replanted.

Years 1 to 3: the immature palm

The first three years are the immature stage, TBM in Indonesian, tanaman belum menghasilkan. The grower pays for seedlings, fertiliser and upkeep with no income from that land. The FAO writes that oil palm begins to bear from the third year, and an IPB University reference (2023) quoting the Indonesian Oil Palm Research Institute puts the palm "ready to produce at age 3".

Breeding shortens the wait. The institute records that its DxP Langkat variety "can begin fruiting at 22 months after planting". Such early bunches are small and seldom harvested; what counts as the first harvest is a bunch worth carrying to the mill, and that arrives towards the third year.

Years 3 to 7: yields climb

Once a palm is in production the curve is steep. The same IPB reference gives a potential of 4 to 6 tonnes of fresh fruit bunches per hectare per year at age three, 12 to 16 tonnes at four, and 15 to 19 tonnes at five. Optimum production runs from age seven to age 25, "yielding 23 to 35 tonnes per hectare per year".

Those are the figures for a well-kept estate planted with certified seedlings. On smallholder land, Indonesia's Ministry of Agriculture said in May 2026 that productivity sits around 2 to 3 tonnes of palm oil per hectare per year against a potential of 4 to 6, and that more than 60 percent of the seedlings in use were not certified.

Years 7 to 25: the peak, then the decline

The FAO describes yield per palm rising gradually with age to a peak around 20 years, then falling from year 25 to year 40, when the palm's economic life is over. Harvesting can continue until the palms are "25 or up to 35 years old" depending on variety and site, but 25 years is the cut-off the Indonesian government uses.

The smallholder replanting programme, PSR, applies exactly that: an estate that "has passed its economic age of 25 years", or whose yield is "10 tonnes of fresh fruit bunches per hectare per year or less at an age of at least 7 years", or that was planted from non-certified seed. There are two roads to replanting: palms that are too old, and palms that never bore as they should.

Why the numbers differ

  • Planting material: certified DxP seedlings fruit sooner and heavier than seedlings raised from picked-up fruit.
  • Water and nutrients: a long dry season and late fertiliser slow growth, and what is lost in the immature years is not made up later.
  • Care in the immature years: fertilising and upkeep in the first years decide how fast a palm reaches production, and they are the first things a tight budget cuts.

Questions people ask

Can oil palm fruit in its second year?
A first bunch can appear, especially on improved varieties that begin at about 22 months. A harvest worth the labour generally starts in the third year.
When should oil palm be replanted?
The Indonesian benchmark is 25 years, or earlier when yield falls below 10 tonnes of bunches per hectare per year at age seven or more. The FAO places the start of the decline at year 25.
How many tonnes per hectare is reasonable?
A good estate: 23 to 35 tonnes of fresh fruit bunches per hectare per year at the peak. Smallholder estates average far below that, around 2 to 3 tonnes of oil per hectare per year according to the Ministry of Agriculture (May 2026).

In the game

Sawit Simulator compresses this timeline so that one estate fits into a few hours. A game day passes every five seconds at normal speed, a game year holds 360 days, and a palm bears in its second year: a seedling needs 540 growing days to mature, and growing days slow down when the block is dry, short of fertiliser or under pest pressure, so two blocks planted on the same day can bear on different days.

Once a palm bears, a harvest round comes every six days, and yield per palm at the peak is deliberately far above a real estate so that a round feels like payday. The age curve follows the real one in shape: rising from year two, flat from age five to 18, falling at 25 and nearly gone at 30. A run ends in year 25 if nothing ends it sooner, so your first estate never gets properly old.
